Video: US humanoid robot plans parkour power with fan in the abdomen twist

THEMIS V2 uses BEAR actuators for smooth, human-like motion with safety, ideal for interacting with people or handling fragile items.

ByJijo Malayil
InnovationApr 07, 2025 09:05 AM EST
ByJijo Malayil
Apr 07, 2025 09:05 AM EST

California-based Westwood Robotics has unveiled its latest humanoid robot, THEMIS V2, a life-sized machine that it claims to be one of the most agile robots of its kind.

Designed to mimic human movement, the upgraded model features major enhancements to its arms and hands. The robot offers 40 degrees of freedom (DoF) and has upgraded 6-DoF arms and advanced 7-DoF end-effectors.

“These upgrades deliver exceptional capabilities in manipulation, perception, and navigation, pushing humanoid robotics to new heights.” says the firm in a YouTube post.

In February, another California-based company, 1X Technologies, unveiled its latest humanoid robot, the NEO Gamma, designed to assist with household tasks.

Lifelike robot evolution

THEMIS V2 is a life-sized humanoid robot standing at 5 feet 3 inches tall with an impressive 40 degrees of freedom, allowing it to move with human-like flexibility.

Major upgrades in this version focus on its arms and hands—its arms now have 6 degrees of freedom, while its hands, or end-effectors, can move in 7 different ways, enabling more precise and versatile tasks.

The humanoid uses Westwood-exclusive BEAR actuators (Back-drivable Electromechanical Actuators for Robotics). These actuators provide the robot the ability to move smoothly and human-like while maintaining safety, which is essential when interacting with people or handling fragile things.

THEMIS V2 can run up to 6.2 mph (10 km/h) and stroll at human speed. It can execute advanced mobility tasks like sprinting, jumping, and even parkour-like actions while keeping balance in unusual situations because it is designed to withstand dynamic surroundings.

Next-gen assistants

Internally, THEMIS V2 is equipped with advanced sensors, including the 3DM-CV7-AHRS from MicroStrain by HBK. By improving orientation and spatial awareness, this sensor allows the robot to react quickly to its changing environment.

The small gadget serves as the robot’s sense of motion and balance, precisely tracking tilts, rotations, and movement speeds up to 1,000 times per second. Similar to a human’s ears and reflexes, it enables the robot to react rapidly to changes in its surroundings, such as navigating uneven ground or dodging obstructions.

In addition, the Robot Operating System (ROS) is a versatile software toolkit that lets programmers modify the robot’s functionality or design new actions. Together, they give the robot both the awareness and adaptability needed for real-world, dynamic interaction.

While Westwood’s Themis targets demanding work sites, 1X Technologies’ Neo Gamma takes on the equally complex challenge of the home. Designed to navigate everyday chaos—rugs, pets, kids, and clutter—Neo is built as a fully autonomous home assistant capable of handling messy, unpredictable tasks without human input.

In real-world demonstrations, Neo has been seen sweeping leaves, loading dishwashers, and rearranging pillows, all without remote control. It uses real-time sensing and decision-making to complete tasks, adjusting dynamically to its surroundings.

In one demo, Neo precisely picks up a cup, transfers it between hands, aligns it with a dishwasher rack, and places it carefully—highlighting its advanced control and coordination.

Another striking moment shows Neo gently placing a cushion on a couch, a task demanding delicate balance and constant adjustments to manage the soft, unstable object.

These seemingly simple actions reveal the robot’s autonomy and its potential to transform domestic assistance.

It’s safe to say that humanoid robots are evolving beyond labs, adapting to real-world tasks and challenges, bringing general-purpose robotic assistants closer to reality.
