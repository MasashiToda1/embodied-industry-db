A robot must ulti­mately act in the phys­i­cal world through con­tin­u­ous per­cep­tion, decision-making, and exe­cu­tion, while coping with vari­a­tions in objects and envi­ron­ments, exe­cu­tion errors, and unex­pected feed­back.\n[2]\n[3]\nThis gives rise to a core chal­lenge that dis­tin­guishes embod­ied intel­li­gence from purely dig­i­tal intel­li­gence:\nhow can gen­eral cog­ni­tive capa­bil­i­ties be trans­lated into reli­able phys­i­cal action?\nPhys­i­cal tasks require models to learn the rela­tion­ships among obser­va­tions, states, actions, and out­comes. A robot must not only iden­tify tar­gets, under­stand spa­tial rela­tion­ships, and plan sequences of oper­a­tions, but also deter­mine whether an action is fea­si­ble, how it will change the envi­ron­ment, and whether the result­ing state matches expec­ta­tions. When a task con­sists of mul­ti­ple inter­de­pen­dent steps, the model must also main­tain task state over time and adapt sub­se­quent deci­sions based on new obser­va­tions and exe­cu­tion feed­back. Embod­ied capa­bil­i­ties there­fore cannot be derived from lan­guage and visual knowl­edge alone. They must also be learned from real or syn­thetic phys­i­cal inter­ac­tion expe­ri­ence,\n[4]\n[5]\nenabling models to cap­ture how the world responds to actions and to turn that expe­ri­ence into exe­cutable behav­ior.\nTo address this chal­lenge, Knowin intro­duces\nGLOW (Gen­er­a­tive Learn­ing of World)\n, a gen­er­a­tive learn­ing frame­work for general-purpose embod­ied intel­li­gence. GLOW is built on real and syn­thetic phys­i­cal inter­ac­tion expe­ri­ence, uses world roll­outs to broaden the model’s expo­sure to pos­si­ble action out­comes, and adopts a uni­fied mul­ti­modal autore­gres­sive for­mu­la­tion that con­nects scene under­stand­ing, phys­i­cal rea­son­ing, task plan­ning, and action gen­er­a­tion. Closed-loop exe­cu­tion then turns model deci­sions into phys­i­cal robot actions. Within GLOW,\nexpe­ri­ence con­struc­tion, world under­stand­ing, model train­ing, action gen­er­a­tion, and exe­cu­tion feed­back\ntogether form a learn­ing loop for acquir­ing phys­i­cal inter­ac­tion capa­bil­i­ties, pro­vid­ing a foun­da­tion for task exe­cu­tion across objects and envi­ron­ments.\nThe goal of GLOW is not to learn a sep­a­rate policy for every task, but to acquire embod­ied capa­bil­i­ties that can be reused across objects, envi­ron­ments, and tasks. Experience Compiler aligns observations, actions, states, contacts, and other training information.\nThe Life Engine com­piles house­hold roles, habits, and the seman­tic struc­ture of the home into a House­hold Pro­gram H, which defines the envi­ron­ment, its oc­cu­pants, their rou­tines and tasks, the cur­rent state, and rel­e­vant events and con­straints. It sim­u­lates events as they unfold over time while main­tain­ing two distinct records: the actual state of the home and the robot’s obser­va­tion his­tory. For exam­ple, when a family member moves a cup outside the robot’s field of view, the world state up­dates imme­di­ately, but the robot’s last recorded obser­va­tion still places the cup at its pre­vi­ous loca­tion. Each expe­ri­ence records event timing, vis­i­bil­ity, and obser­va­tion sources, help­ing the model learn to assess whether past obser­va­tions remain valid and deter­mine when and where to seek up­dat­ed infor­ma­tion.\nThe Physics Engine turns the House­hold Pro­gram H pro­duced by Life Engine into an exe­cutable 3D envi­ron­ment and sim­u­lates how actions and events change that envi­ron­ment over time. Every object in H becomes a 3D body with geom­e­try, phys­i­cal prop­er­ties, and joints, and every action has to be phys­i­cal­ly pos­si­ble: “put the cup in the cab­i­net” proceeds only if the cup is reach­able, the door is open, and the grasp holds. The result­ing motion and con­tacts come from physics sim­u­la­tion rather than being written into the pro­gram, and their effects persist—an opened door stays open and limits what the robot can reach next. The output is a Grounded Tra­jec­to­ry τ: a time-aligned record of actions, con­tacts, and object states in which every step is phys­i­cal­ly fea­si­ble and every label is read di­rect­ly from the sim­u­la­tion, so it is cor­rect by con­struc­tion.\nThe Dream Engine renders Pho­to­real Expe­ri­ence X around τ and varies it in appear­ance (light­ing, mate­ri­als, back­grounds), object (struc­turally com­pat­i­ble sub­sti­tutes, re-executed in the Physics Engine), and scene (home, camera view, robot con­fig­u­ra­tion), while the inter­ac­tion itself stays the same.
